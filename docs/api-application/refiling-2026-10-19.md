## Why

The approved application says two things the product now needs to change:

1. **Scope.** We hold `portability.activity.single/.ongoing`. TikTok's Data Types page (updated
   2026-08-28) lists *Favourite Videos* only in the full archive, under "Likes and Favourites", not in
   Activity. If that holds, our scope never delivers the one thing Stash reads.
2. **Frequency.** We declared "at most one data request per connected user per 24 h". New: a request
   when the user opens Stash, at most once every 15 minutes, never in the background.

## Form answers (if TikTok says "reapply")

**Purpose:** Portability (unchanged).

**Scopes:** Variant A `portability.all.single`, `portability.all.ongoing`. Variant B the two Activity
scopes as today. The form wants a separate explanation per scope; a shared one is rejected.

**`portability.all.single` — feature that needs it** (Variant A)

> The first import when a user connects TikTok to Stash. Stash turns the user's Favourite Videos into
> a searchable personal library (recipes, music, how-to summaries). Favourite Videos are delivered
> only in the full archive ("Likes and Favourites"); no narrower category contains them, so this is the
> narrowest scope that reaches them. From the archive Stash reads only the Favourite Videos entries
> (date, video link). Every other category is discarded in memory without being read or logged, and
> the archive is deleted immediately after extraction. Mockups: screens 2–4.

**`portability.all.ongoing` — feature that needs it** (Variant A)

> Keeping the library in step with the user's new bookmarks after the first import, without asking
> them to re-authorise. Each transfer returns the full list, so Stash compares it with what the user
> already has and adds only new favourites. Same minimisation as the single scope: favourites only,
> everything else discarded in memory, archive deleted at once. Mockup: screen 3.

**How often will your application access the selected data scope**

> One time and ongoing. One request when the user connects TikTok, then one request when the user
> opens Stash, at most once every 15 minutes per user, and never in the background. At most one
> request per user is in flight at a time; while TikTok is preparing an archive, Stash sends no new
> request. A user who does not open the app generates no requests.
>
> Why continuous access is necessary: users bookmark a video in TikTok and then open Stash to find it,
> usually within minutes. A single transfer would freeze the library at the day of connecting, and a
> daily refresh means the video they just saved is missing when they look for it. Tying requests to
> app use, rather than a timer, keeps the volume to what the user actually asks for.

## As filed 2026-10-26

**Feature that requires All data**

> Stash is a consumer iOS app on the App Store (Apple ID 6789977520). It turns each user's own TikTok
> Favourite Videos into a searchable personal library: recipe cards, track lists and how-to summaries.
> Favourite Videos are delivered only in the full archive ("Likes and Favourites"); no narrower
> category contains them. Our first live transfer under our approved Activity scopes (19 October 2026)
> returned no Favourite Videos, so All is the narrowest scope that reaches them. From the archive Stash
> reads only the Favourite Videos entries (date and video link). Every other category is discarded in
> memory without being read or logged, and the archive file is deleted immediately after extraction.
> Nothing else is stored. Each transfer returns the full list, so Stash compares it with what the user
> already has and adds only new favourites. Offered only to users we identify as EEA/UK from their App
> Store storefront. Mockups: screens 2-4. This application replaces our Activity application approved
> on 7 October 2026.

**Why the frequency is necessary** — the "How often" answer above, without its first sentence, with
"a daily refresh" changed to "our previously declared daily refresh".

**How users delete data in the app**

> Settings > Delete account (behind a confirmation step) immediately removes the user's account, their
> library including every favourite imported from TikTok, quota counters and import bookkeeping from
> our servers, deletes the copy on the phone, and revokes Stash's access to the user's TikTok account.
> Settings > Disconnect TikTok revokes Stash's access at TikTok and deletes the stored tokens, which
> stops all further transfers. TikTok archives are never kept: each one is deleted from our server as
> soon as the Favourite Videos entries are read. Users who want a single video removed can also email
> privacy@stash.dmitrijs.dev.

**Data protection policy and processes**

> Our privacy policy (https://stash.dmitrijs.dev/privacy) documents what we hold, our sub-processors
> (Amazon Web Services in eu-north-1 and eu-central-1; Groq and OpenRouter for transient
> speech-to-text and caption analysis, under Standard Contractual Clauses), retention periods, and how
> users exercise their rights. Controls for TikTok data: the archive is processed in memory; only
> Favourite Videos entries (date, video link) are kept; every other category is discarded without
> being stored or logged; the archive file is deleted as soon as those entries are read. Each user's
> data is stored under their own account and shown only to that user. TikTok tokens are held only on
> our server and deleted on disconnect or account deletion. Everything between the app and our server
> travels over HTTPS; the database and processing queue are encrypted at rest; administrative access
> is by SSH key from a single operator address, and only the operator can access production data. We
> hold no ISO, SOC 2 or CASA certification.
